# Ranked Shortlist of LLM Judge Models for a Leakage-Detection Autograder

## TL;DR
- **Re-host GLM-5.2 on Fireworks AI (not Z.ai) as the primary open-weight candidate.** GLM already cleared your own 47-row bar (TNR 1.00/0.97, TPR 1.0); its only failure was Z.ai's serving layer, and multiple sources confirm the stalls are an infrastructure/capacity problem — not the model — that goes away on Western hosts that serve the same MIT-licensed weights with a controlled path and uptime SLA.
- **Pair it with a cross-family reasoning judge — DeepSeek-R1 (MIT) or Llama-Nemotron LN-Ultra — as the #2 candidate,** because reasoning/test-time compute is the single model-side property most correlated with high JudgeBench accuracy and the careful "count the surviving options" specificity your CLEAN carve-out demands; LN-Ultra is the strongest open-weight judge on JudgeBench (79.14 Overall, behind only o3-mini-high at 80.86), with DeepSeek-R1 next at 73.14.
- **Add Qwen3-235B-A22B (Apache 2.0) as the #3 / ensemble anchor** for its best-in-class strict-JSON reliability and cleanest license — but for ALL three, the binding risk to your zero-flip cert is MoE batch-nondeterminism at temperature 0, which forces you toward self-hosting or dedicated (non-shared-batch) endpoints, not shared serverless.

## Key Findings

1. **Your own 47-row result is stronger evidence than any public benchmark.** No public LLM-as-judge benchmark measures your exact task (leakage detection with a specificity-first CLEAN carve-out). GLM-5.2's measured TNR 1.00/0.97 on your frozen set already meets the intent of the bar; the cert is blocked only by Z.ai stalls. That makes "fix GLM's hosting" a higher-probability path to certification than "swap to a fresh model and re-validate from scratch."

2. **Alternative hosting demonstrably fixes GLM's reliability problem.** The stalls are a Z.ai serving-layer/capacity issue, not a weight problem. Community and independent write-ups confirm GLM itself "remains capable" and that "other GLM providers offer the same model with reliable infrastructure." Z.ai's own GitHub carries a critical issue documenting ~2 consecutive days of near-total outage from rate limiting, acknowledged as "a capacity issue, not a per-account problem." Fireworks serves GLM-5.2 in-house (not as a router) with a "fully controlled serving path, a zero-data-retention policy, and the uptime SLA your enterprise requires," and independently reproduced Z.ai's GPQA-Diamond numbers (91.4% on Fireworks' own engine vs 91.2% reported), implying minimal quality drift.

3. **Watch quantization when you re-host.** DeepInfra serves GLM in fp4 (4-bit), which is not bit-identical to reference weights and can shift borderline judge decisions — exactly the kind of drift that can flip a run below the TNR floor. Fireworks runs GLM on its own engine and validated benchmarks against reference, making it the lower-risk host for a specificity-sensitive judge. This is a per-candidate hosting decision, not a model decision.

4. **Reasoning ability is the best model-side predictor of judge specificity — with caveats.** JudgeBench is deliberately hard: top reward models reach only ~64% accuracy, and GPT-4o with a vanilla prompt scores just 50.9% Overall — "barely above random guessing except in mathematics" (Tan et al., ICLR 2025, arXiv:2410.12784). Reasoning models dominate: o3-mini scores 80.86%, 76.57% and 70.57% at high/medium/low reasoning effort respectively — a clean monotonic climb. Per NVIDIA's Llama-Nemotron report (arXiv:2505.00949, Table 6), LN-Ultra "emerges as the best open-source model, significantly surpassing DeepSeek-R1 and trailing only behind o3-mini(high)," with JudgeBench Overall scores of **LN-Ultra 79.14, DeepSeek-R1 73.14, o3-mini(high) 80.86**. BUT reasoning is not a guarantee of specificity: in C2-Faith (arXiv:2603.05167), DeepSeek-V3.1 posted the highest detection (94.7%) but "partly driven by a high false positive rate (29.6%)," and reasoning gave no benefit on out-of-distribution judging tasks.

5. **Structured rubrics + strict JSON schema are a bigger lever on false-positive rate than model choice.** Rubric-based evaluation studies find that expert rubrics sharply raise accuracy and cut false-positive rate, while removing that structure both lowers accuracy and multiplies FPR. Your two-revision rubric with the explicit CLEAN carve-out and "count the surviving options" decision test is doing the heavy lifting; model capacity must be sufficient to *apply* it, but the rubric is why your TNR can reach ≥0.95 at all. (Note: the specific "88.9%/6.2% FPR" magnitudes I saw could not be tied to a single named source and should be treated as directional.)

6. **Strict-JSON reliability varies by model AND serving stack.** Qwen3-235B is one of the most reliable open models for structured output (OpenRouter measured ~87.97% raw valid JSON rising to 99.98% with response-healing), and grammar-constrained decoding (XGrammar/LLGuidance on vLLM/SGLang, or Fireworks/DeepInfra JSON-schema modes) can guarantee schema conformance — which directly serves your "abstain, never fail open" design. Two gotchas: (a) Qwen3 "thinking" modes have documented guided-decoding bugs where JSON breaks when `enable_thinking=False`, and reasoning content can leak into `message.content`; (b) on Fireworks, using `response_format` with a JSON schema disables reasoning output, so reasoning judges need the schema in the prompt instead.

7. **Every strong candidate is a Mixture-of-Experts model, so temperature-0 is NOT bit-deterministic across runs.** MoE routing plus batch-size-dependent reduction kernels make temp-0 outputs vary run-to-run on shared endpoints — precisely the "judges drift run-to-run even at temperature 0" you observed, and the direct threat to your zero-flip requirement. This is now solvable: Thinking Machines Lab's batch-invariant kernels produced "bit-identical outputs across 1,000 repeated runs" (originally at ~61.5% throughput cost; the SGLang team integrated them with CUDA graphs, reducing overhead to ~34.35%) — but only if you control the serving stack.

8. **Cross-family judging is the documented mitigation for self-enhancement bias, and open-weight judges give it to you for free.** Models systematically favor their own and same-family outputs — Spiliopoulou et al. ("Play Favorites," arXiv:2508.06709, >5000 prompt-completion pairs across 9 judges) found "GPT-4o and Claude 3.5 Sonnet systematically assign higher scores to their own outputs" and "also display family-bias." If your coach is Claude/GPT-generated, an open-weight GLM/DeepSeek/Qwen judge is automatically cross-family. The corollary: never judge a GLM-authored coach with a GLM judge.

## Details

### Candidate 1 — GLM-5.2, re-hosted on Fireworks AI (TOP open-weight pick)
- **Judge-quality evidence:** Your own empirical result is the primary evidence: TNR 1.00/0.97, TPR 1.0 on the 47-row set. Public judge-benchmark data specifically for GLM-5/5.2 is a genuine gap — no JudgeBench/RewardBench judge score is published for the GLM-5 series. General reasoning is frontier-class among open weights: GPQA-Diamond ~80.3% (Fireworks/Z.ai report 91.2–91.4% at high reasoning effort), and top open-weight on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index v4.1 (51). Interpretation: strong general reasoning is necessary-but-not-sufficient for the carve-out; your direct measurement is what de-risks it.
- **Structured-output reliability:** GLM supports OpenAI/Anthropic-compatible endpoints; Fireworks and DeepInfra both offer JSON-schema/grammar-constrained decoding. Your abstain-on-missing-field design is well supported by grammar-constrained decoding, which forces required fields.
- **Hosting/reliability:** This is the whole point. Z.ai stalls are serving-layer/capacity issues ("this isn't a model problem, it's an infrastructure problem" per an independent March 2026 write-up; Z.ai GitHub issue #83 documents ~2 days of near-total outage from rate limiting). Fireworks serves in-house with ZDR + uptime SLA and validated GLM-5.2 benchmarks against reference. GLM-5.2 is listed across ~20 providers via OpenRouter (29 distinct listings on 3 July 2026).
- **License:** MIT (unrestricted commercial use, self-hosting, redistribution).
- **Rough per-call cost:** Fireworks/Z.ai list ~$1.40/$4.40 per 1M in/out; discounted routes ~$0.93–$1.20 in (DeepInfra fp4 floor). For a judge call (~2–3k input tokens: passage+question+4 options+coach reply+rubric; ~200-token JSON out plus reasoning), that is roughly $0.005–$0.02 per verdict.
- **Temp-0/determinism:** temp-0 capable, but 744B MoE (40B active) → batch-nondeterminism on shared endpoints; needs dedicated endpoint or self-host for zero-flip.
- **Cross-family:** Fine unless your coach is also GLM. If coach is GLM-authored, disqualify to avoid self-preference bias.
- **Key risk:** DeepInfra's fp4 serving of GLM can drift borderline decisions; prefer Fireworks (own-engine, reference-validated) for the judge.

### Candidate 2 — DeepSeek-R1 (or Llama-Nemotron LN-Ultra) — cross-family reasoning judge
- **Judge-quality evidence:** LN-Ultra is the best open-source model on JudgeBench at 79.14 Overall, "significantly surpassing DeepSeek-R1" (73.14) and "trailing only behind o3-mini(high)" (80.86) (arXiv:2505.00949, Table 6). Reasoning effort correlates monotonically with judge accuracy. DeepSeek-R1 is MIT-licensed and widely hosted (Fireworks, DeepInfra, Together); it is described as "determinism-friendly when you set temperature low and define formats."
- **Why it fits your task:** the CLEAN carve-out requires genuine reasoning ("count the surviving options") rather than pattern-matching "teaching language → leak" — the exact OVERFLAG-1 failure that sank Haiku. Reasoning judges are the model class most likely to apply that test correctly.
- **Structured-output reliability:** DeepSeek-R1 supports reasoning-then-JSON; Fireworks documents a Reasoning-JSON pattern (schema in prompt so reasoning is preserved; parse `<think>…</think>` separately). Constrained decoding available on self-host and major hosts.
- **Hosting/reliability:** Multiple no-train Western hosts; DeepInfra is SOC 2 + ISO 27001 with a zero-retention policy; Fireworks offers a 99.9% uptime SLA + ZDR.
- **License:** DeepSeek-R1/V3.2 MIT; Llama-Nemotron under NVIDIA's open model license (verify terms before production).
- **Rough per-call cost:** DeepSeek is among the cheapest frontier open weights (V4-Flash first-party ~$0.14/$0.28 per 1M; R1/V3.2 similarly low). Per verdict likely well under $0.01, though reasoning tokens inflate output.
- **Temp-0/determinism:** MoE → same batch-nondeterminism caveat; low temp + fixed format + dedicated/self-host recommended.
- **Cross-family:** Strong cross-family choice against Claude/GPT/GLM-authored coaches.
- **Caveats:** high detection can hide high FPR in some reasoning models (DeepSeek-V3.1 detection partly driven by a 29.6% FPR) — you MUST re-run your 47-row cert; reasoning gives no free lunch OOD. Verbose reasoning raises latency/cost and truncation risk (budget ≥4k output tokens).

### Candidate 3 — Qwen3-235B-A22B (Apache 2.0) — JSON-reliability + ensemble anchor
- **Judge-quality evidence:** Used as a judge baseline in the literature (e.g., as a "mixture-of-judges" ground-truth voter alongside DeepSeek-R1 and DeepSeek-V3), but a 7B specialist (CompassJudger-2) is reported to match it, so it is a *capable but not exceptional* stand-alone judge. Best used where structured-output reliability and license cleanliness matter, or as the third vote in an ensemble.
- **Structured-output reliability:** Among the best — OpenRouter measured ~87.97% raw valid JSON rising to 99.98% with healing; broad tooling/quantization ecosystem.
- **Hosting/reliability:** Widely hosted (DeepInfra, Together, Groq, Alibaba DashScope) and easy to self-host on vLLM/SGLang.
- **License:** Apache 2.0 — the most permissive of the shortlist (no MAU/revenue clauses).
- **Rough per-call cost:** Cheapest of the three; DashScope from ~$0.15/M input, self-host on a single/dual H100 feasible for the 235B-A22B MoE (22B active).
- **Temp-0/determinism:** MoE, so same caveat; but small active-parameter count and mature vLLM/SGLang support make batch-invariant self-hosting most practical here.
- **Cross-family:** Cross-family vs Claude/GPT/GLM/DeepSeek.
- **Caveats:** thinking-mode guided-decoding bugs (`enable_thinking=False` can break JSON; reasoning leaking into content) — pin a known-good serving config and test JSON validity before cert.

### Retained baseline — GPT-4o (proprietary)
Keep as the proven reference and as an ensemble/tie-break anchor. It is your only model clean on all axes (TNR 1.00, TPR 1.0, temp-0 capable, reliable), but it is proprietary and self-preference-biased if the coach is GPT-authored. Use it to label disagreements while you certify an open-weight replacement.

### Cross-cutting: the temperature-0 / zero-flip problem
Your zero-flip floor is the hardest constraint for any MoE judge. Setting temperature=0 only makes token *sampling* deterministic; batch-size-dependent reduction kernels and MoE routing still cause run-to-run drift on shared serverless endpoints (Anthropic itself notes temp-0 is "not fully deterministic"). Mitigations, in order of robustness: (1) self-host on vLLM/SGLang with batch-invariant kernels (bit-identical across 1,000 runs demonstrated; ~34% throughput cost via SGLang); (2) use a dedicated (single-tenant, fixed-batch) endpoint from Fireworks/DeepInfra rather than shared serverless; (3) at minimum, run more than 3 replays and treat any flip as a hard fail. Grammar-constrained decoding also shrinks the output space, reducing the chance a numeric-noise perturbation changes the final boolean.

## Recommendations

**Stage 1 (now): Fix the incumbent.** Re-run your exact 47-row cert on **GLM-5.2 via Fireworks AI** (own-engine, ZDR, uptime SLA, reference-validated). Use a JSON-schema/grammar-constrained decode so missing fields force an abstain, not a fail-open. Success criterion: ≥3 temp-0 replays all clearing TNR ≥0.95, TPR ≥0.90, κ ≥0.75, zero-flip. If it passes, you have a certified open-weight judge with minimal re-validation risk. **Avoid DeepInfra's fp4 GLM route for the judge** — reserve fp4 for cost-bound telemetry, not the gating verdict.

**Stage 2 (in parallel): Certify a cross-family reasoning judge.** Stand up **DeepSeek-R1 (MIT)** — or **LN-Ultra** if you want the top open JudgeBench score (79.14) — on a dedicated Fireworks/DeepInfra endpoint or self-host. Put the schema in the prompt (so reasoning is preserved), budget ≥4k output tokens, and run the same cert. This gives you a non-GLM, non-Claude/GPT judge that is architecturally suited to the "count the surviving options" carve-out and protects you if the coach model family changes.

**Stage 3 (determinism hardening): Move the winning judge to a batch-invariant self-host** (vLLM/SGLang with batch-invariant kernels) if zero-flip fails on shared/dedicated endpoints. This is the only way to *guarantee* the zero-flip floor for an MoE judge.

**Stage 4 (optional, highest robustness): Cross-family ensemble.** Run GLM-5.2 + DeepSeek-R1 + Qwen3-235B and gate on unanimous `answer_leakage=false` for CLEAN (raising specificity) or majority vote (raising κ). Add GPT-4o as a tie-breaker only. Ensembles are the documented mitigation for self-preference and single-model drift, at ~3× cost/latency — justified only once leakage gates the live coach.

**Thresholds that would change the plan:**
- If GLM-5.2/Fireworks flips below TNR 0.95 on any replay → promote DeepSeek-R1/LN-Ultra to primary.
- If a reasoning judge shows high TPR but TNR <0.95 (the OVERFLAG-1 / high-FPR pattern, as seen with DeepSeek-V3.1's 29.6% FPR) → it is over-flagging; tighten the rubric's CLEAN examples before changing models.
- If per-call latency/cost from reasoning tokens is unacceptable at production volume → use a constrained-decode non-reasoning judge for telemetry and reserve the reasoning judge for the gating decision only.
- Choose Qwen3-235B (Apache 2.0) over GLM/DeepSeek if license cleanliness or a single-H100 self-host footprint becomes the deciding factor.

## Caveats
- **No benchmark measures your exact task.** JudgeBench/RewardBench measure pairwise correctness and preference, not leakage-detection specificity with a CLEAN carve-out. Treat them as directional proxies; your 47-row human-α set remains the ground truth.
- **Reasoning ≠ guaranteed specificity.** Some reasoning judges trade higher recall for higher false-positive rates (DeepSeek-V3.1: 94.7% detection but 29.6% FPR), and reasoning gives no benefit out-of-distribution. Every model must re-pass your cert; do not port a benchmark ranking into a go/no-go.
- **MoE temp-0 nondeterminism is real and is your biggest cert risk.** Shared serverless endpoints will likely flip run-to-run; plan for dedicated or self-hosted batch-invariant serving.
- **Quantization changes judge behavior.** fp4/fp8 serving (common on cheap routes like DeepInfra's fp4 GLM) is not reference-equivalent; pin precision and re-validate whenever you change host or quant.
- **Public GLM-5 judge data is thin.** GLM has no published JudgeBench/RewardBench judge score; you are relying on your own measurement plus general reasoning benchmarks. That is acceptable given your direct evidence, but note it. Clean standalone JudgeBench numbers for Qwen3-235B and DeepSeek-V3.2 as judges are also not well-established in public sources.
- **Some 2026 model/version and pricing figures come from vendor and reseller pages** in a fast-moving landscape; verify current price, license text, quantization, and SLA on the provider's own page before committing, and be skeptical of vendor "best/uptime" self-claims (one source lists Fireworks at 99.9% uptime, another at 99.99%).
- **License fine print:** MIT/Apache are clean, but some open models (e.g., Kimi K2's Modified MIT) add branding clauses above MAU/revenue thresholds; LN-Ultra uses NVIDIA's open model license — read the exact model-card LICENSE before production.
