# Designing a Gold-Standard Calibration Dataset and Rubric for a Binary "GoalJudge" LLM-as-Judge

## TL;DR
- **Use a trajectory-aware judge, but anchor it to checkable evidence (a hybrid "outcome-grounded, trajectory-aware" design).** The best controlled head-to-head (Agent-as-a-Judge, Zhuge et al., arXiv 2410.10934, ICML 2025) found its trajectory-aware judge "aligns more closely with the consensus of our human judges (90%) as compared to LLM-as-a-Judge (70%) in all cases tested" — but trajectory-aware judges are dangerously gameable: "Gaming the Judge" (arXiv 2601.14691, Jan 2026) shows that "manipulated reasoning alone can inflate false positive rates of state-of-the-art VLM judges by up to 90% across 800 trajectories." The resolution is to feed the judge the trajectory *and* the final answer but require every "goal_met=True" verdict to be grounded in observable state/tool outputs, not in the agent's narration — exactly what that paper recommends ("judging mechanisms that verify reasoning claims against observable evidence").
- **No public dataset gives ready-made binary goal_met labels for a generic ReAct pipeline.** The closest reusable assets are τ-bench/τ²-bench (deterministic end-state goal labels), WebArena (programmatic functional-correctness + unachievable tasks), TheAgentCompany (checkpoint partial-credit), SWE-bench Verified (human-validated pass/fail), AgentRewardBench (1,302 expert-labeled web-agent success trajectories), and TRAIL/MAST (graded trajectory error taxonomies, not goal_met). Reuse their *schemas and ground-truth definitions*, then build your own few-hundred-item gold set.
- **For a binary goal_met gold set: decompose goals into atomic checkable success conditions; score correctly-reported-impossible tasks as goal_met=False (correct behavior ≠ goal achieved, tracked on a separate axis); threshold partial completion conservatively to False; target Krippendorff's α ≥ 0.8; and report judge calibration as precision/recall/F1 on the goal_met=False class (the class that triggers your downgrade) plus ECE.** Plan ~200–300 stratified, double-labeled items for initial calibration (~250 validates 80% agreement at 95% confidence).

## Key Findings

### The unit-of-judgment trade-off (primary question)
1. **Trajectory-aware judging agrees with humans better — on honest trajectories.** Agent-as-a-Judge (arXiv 2410.10934, ICML 2025) directly compared a "black box" output-only judge against a "gray box" trajectory-aware agent-judge on the DevAI benchmark (55 tasks, 365 requirements). Verbatim: it "aligns more closely with the consensus of our human judges (90%) as compared to LLM-as-a-Judge (70%) in all cases tested," while "sav[ing] 97.72% of the time and 97.64% of the cost compared to involving three human experts" (86h/$1,297 of human effort reduced to ~2h/$31).
2. **But trajectory-aware judging is gameable and biases toward rewarded effort/narration.** "Gaming the Judge" (Khalifa et al., arXiv 2601.14691) rewrote *only* the agent's chain-of-thought while holding actions and observations fixed: "manipulated reasoning alone can inflate false positive rates of state-of-the-art VLM judges by up to 90% across 800 trajectories spanning diverse web tasks." Content-based manipulations (fabricating progress) were worst; the average judgment-flip rate (correct FAILURE → SUCCESS) reached ~35.8%, and in a live setup confident/manipulative reasoning inflated judged success by +19.6 to +22.9 absolute points over true success. Stronger-grounding models (o4-mini, gpt-5-mini) were most robust; Qwen-2.5-VL-72B and GLM-4.1V were least. Mitigations (warnings, rubric methods, judge-time scaling) reduce but do not eliminate the effect, and robustness comes at the cost of recall. The paper's conclusion is the design north star: build "judging mechanisms that verify reasoning claims against observable evidence."
3. **Output-only judging misses invalid-process / right-answer-wrong-reason failures.** Eval frameworks (LangChain, MLflow/Phoenix, Galileo) note output-only evaluation cannot catch an agent that "arrives at the right answer through incorrect reasoning," fabricated tool outputs, or unsafe/wasteful paths.
4. **End-state / state-matching evaluation is the most robust objective signal where it's available.** τ-bench (Yao et al., arXiv 2406.12045, Sierra) computes reward by comparing the database end-state to an annotated goal state: "Success is determined by a deterministic comparison of end database state with an annotated goal state, irrespective of the conversational trajectory." This yields goal_met with no LLM judging and no trajectory bias, but only covers goals that map to inspectable state.
5. **Process-reward vs. outcome-reward literature says neither dominates universally.** ViLBench (arXiv 2503.20271) finds "neither ORM nor PRM consistently outperforms across all tasks"; "Lessons of Developing PRMs" (arXiv 2501.07301) documents a "process-to-outcome shift." Implication: process information helps localize *why* a run failed but should not be the sole basis for the *whether* (goal_met) decision.
6. **The latest practitioner consensus is explicitly hybrid.** 2026 practitioner writing (LangChain, Galileo, Adnan Masood's rubric survey) converges on: start with outcome checks, add trajectory checks for critical/high-stakes workflows, and use analytic (criterion-by-criterion) rubrics rather than a single holistic score.

### Available gold datasets (catalog)
- **τ-bench / τ²-bench (arXiv 2406.12045 / 2506.07982, Sierra):** Tool-Agent-User conversations (retail, airline). Ground truth = deterministic comparison of database end-state to annotated goal state, plus required output strings; reward r ∈ {0,1}. Full trajectories included; pass^k reliability metric; MIT-licensed code. The repo also ships an auto-error-identification module (fault assignment to user/agent/environment; fault types incl. goal_partially_completed, used_wrong_tool, used_wrong_tool_argument). **Best conceptual fit for goal_met.**
- **WebArena / VisualWebArena (arXiv 2307.13854 / 2401.13649):** 812 long-horizon web tasks; functional-correctness reward functions (exact_match, must_include, fuzzy GPT-judge for info tasks; programmatic state predicates for nav/config). Explicitly includes **unachievable tasks** to test impossibility recognition. Trajectories available. (Note: the "WebArena Verified" critique shows the harness over-credits premature "N/A" answers on unachievable tasks — a cautionary tale for impossible-task scoring.)
- **GAIA (arXiv 2311.12983):** 466 real-world assistant questions, single correct answer, quasi-exact-match auto-eval; humans 92% vs. GPT-4+plugins 15%. Explicitly "does not evaluate the trace." Outputs only.
- **SWE-bench / SWE-bench Verified (OpenAI, Aug 13 2024):** 500 human-validated GitHub-issue tasks; ground truth = FAIL_TO_PASS / PASS_TO_PASS unit tests. OpenAI "worked with 93 software developers experienced in Python to manually screen SWE-bench samples" (38.3% flagged for underspecified problems, 61.1% for unfair tests, 68.3% filtered out). Binary pass/fail; GPT-4o resolved 33.2%. **Caveat:** AI Wiki reports Verified was "deprecated... on February 23, 2026 due to test flaws and training-data contamination," with OpenAI pointing to the contamination-resistant SWE-bench Pro (built by Scale AI).
- **TheAgentCompany (arXiv 2412.14161, CMU):** 175 consequential workplace tasks; checkpoint-based scoring — S_full = 1 iff all checkpoints pass, S_partial = 0.5·(points/total) + 0.5·S_full. Deterministic Python evaluators + LLM evaluators for subjective deliverables. Full trajectories. **Best model for partial-completion thresholding.** Best agent (Gemini 2.5 Pro) completed 30.3% fully / 39.3% partial.
- **AgentBench (arXiv 2308.03688, ICLR 2024):** 8 environments, success-rate/reward/F1. Outcome-focused; trajectories available.
- **AgentBoard (arXiv 2401.13178, NeurIPS 2024):** 1,013 environments with manually annotated subgoals and a fine-grained "progress rate" metric. **Best model for goal decomposition.**
- **AgentRewardBench (Lù, Kazemnejad, Meade et al., arXiv 2504.08942, McGill/Mila):** "AgentRewardBench contains 1302 trajectories across 5 benchmarks and 4 LLMs," each expert-reviewed for success/side-effects/repetitiveness; "we evaluate 12 LLM judges and find that no single LLM excels across all benchmarks," and rule-based methods "severely underestimate the capabilities of agents and do not reflect how experts define success." **Closest existing LLM-judge-calibration set for agent success.**
- **TRAIL (arXiv 2505.08638, Patronus):** 148 annotated traces (118 GAIA + 30 SWE-bench), 841 errors, graded **span/turn-level** taxonomy (reasoning/planning/execution × Low/Med/High impact). NOT binary goal_met — it is error localization/debugging. ~94–95% span-level review agreement (5.63%/5.31% spans modified); a chance-corrected κ was not reported. Best model (Gemini-2.5-Pro) scored only 11%. License bars use for training automated human-evaluation systems.
- **MAST / MAST-Data (arXiv 2503.13657):** 1,642 annotated multi-agent traces, 14 failure modes in 3 categories; taxonomy κ = 0.88 across three annotators; LLM-as-judge annotator reached 94% accuracy / κ = 0.77 vs. humans. Failure-mode labels, not goal_met.
- **JudgeBench (arXiv 2410.12784):** Pairwise judge meta-eval on objectively verifiable response pairs (knowledge/reasoning/math/coding); strong models barely beat random. Tests judges, not agents — useful for judge-robustness practices.
- **PayPal Proxy State-Based Evaluation (Chuang et al., arXiv 2602.16246):** An "LLM-driven simulation framework that preserves final state-based evaluation without a deterministic database." An LLM state-tracker infers a structured proxy state from the full trace; LLM judges verify goal completion and detect tool/user hallucinations against scenario constraints; reported human–LLM judge agreement exceeds 90%. **Closest 2026 design to a trajectory-aware goal_met judge for industrial tool-calling agents.**
- **AbstentionBench (arXiv 2506.09038):** 20 datasets / 35,000+ unanswerable queries; metric is abstention recall. Directly relevant to the impossible-task edge case; notably finds reasoning fine-tuning *degrades* abstention by ~24%.

### Rubric and annotation-protocol best practices
- **Anchor the binary rubric to checkable evidence.** Decompose each goal into atomic, self-contained success conditions (à la AgentBoard subgoals / WebArena programmatic checks / TheAgentCompany checkpoints); goal_met=True iff all required atomic conditions are verified against observable state/tool outputs, not agent narration.
- **Correctly-reported-impossible tasks:** Gold datasets (WebArena unachievable tasks; AbstentionBench) treat the *desired outcome* and the *agent behavior* as separate axes. For goal attainment, a correctly-reported-impossible task is **goal_met=False** (the user's goal was not achieved) but carries a separate "correct_behavior/graceful_failure=True" tag so it is not conflated with hallucinated/failed runs. Graceful failure must never count as goal achievement.
- **Partial completion:** Under a binary scheme, threshold conservatively. TheAgentCompany shows partial-credit scoring is informative but full completion must be strongly and separately incentivized; for a binary goal_met that gates a downgrade, partial = False, with the partial fraction recorded as metadata.
- **IAA targets:** Krippendorff's α ≥ 0.8 is the common "rule of thumb" for reliable datasets (α ≥ 0.667 as a tentative-conclusions floor); Cohen's κ ≥ 0.6 is a frequent practical bar for judge–human agreement; high-stakes domains (e.g., clinical) often require α/κ > 0.9. Use Cohen's κ for two fixed annotators; Krippendorff's α for multi-annotator/missing-data settings.
- **Calibration metrics:** Report raw agreement and κ, **precision/recall/F1 specifically on the goal_met=False class** (since that triggers the downgrade), and Expected Calibration Error (ECE) for any confidence outputs — but note LLM-judge confidence is often severely miscalibrated (studies report ECE in the 0.11–0.43 range), so use ECE diagnostically rather than as a trusted probability.
- **Size:** ~200–300 labeled items is the practitioner-consensus floor for calibration; validating 80% human–LLM agreement at 95% confidence needs ~246–250 samples (binomial proportion). Split dev/test (~60/40) and avoid iterating the rubric/prompt on the test split (overfitting risk).
- **Stratification:** Stratify by domain, difficulty, success vs. failure, and explicitly oversample the goal_met=False and impossible-task strata (the downgrade decision depends on them). A standard gold-set composition is ~40% representative / 30% boundary / 20% edge / 10% (here) impossible cases; watch overall class balance.

## Details

### A. Final-output-only vs. trajectory-aware: full trade-off analysis

**Accuracy / agreement with humans.** The best controlled comparison (Agent-as-a-Judge on DevAI) shows trajectory-aware 90% vs. output-only 70% agreement with expert consensus; PayPal's proxy-state work independently reports >90% human–LLM agreement using a trajectory-derived state representation. On *honestly produced* trajectories, more context helps — chiefly because output-only judging cannot verify whether the answer was achieved or merely asserted.

**Bias and gaming (the decisive caveat).** "Gaming the Judge" proves trajectory-aware judges reward *how reasoning is presented* over *what was achieved*: progress-fabricating or competence-boosting CoT inflates judged success by ~20–23 absolute points (true success unchanged) and false-positive rates by up to 90% in the controlled setting. This is precisely the "rewarding effort/process over outcome" failure to avoid, and it is empirically severe. The effective mitigation is to force the judge to verify reasoning claims against observable evidence (state, tool outputs, screenshots).

**Calibration / label noise / reproducibility.** Deterministic end-state checks (τ-bench) are the most reproducible and lowest-noise but exist only where goals map to inspectable state. LLM judges (either input mode) add stochasticity and miscalibrated confidence; trajectory-aware judging adds more input variance (long traces — MAST traces average >15k tokens — plus ordering effects). Output-only judging is cheaper, faster, more reproducible, and less gameable via CoT, but blind to invalid process and fabricated success.

**Cost / latency.** Output-only: low token cost, low latency. Trajectory-aware: high token cost, higher latency, and added infrastructure to build/maintain reference trajectories (LangChain explicitly notes this overhead is "not always worth it").

**Recommendation (evidence-based).**
- **Default for GoalJudge: trajectory-aware but outcome-grounded.** Give the judge the goal, the full trajectory (tool calls + tool *outputs* + intermediate states) and the final answer. In the rubric, instruct it to (a) base goal_met only on verifiable state/tool-output evidence, (b) explicitly distrust agent self-narration of progress, and (c) emit the supporting evidence span for each satisfied success condition *before* deciding.
- **Where goals map to checkable state, prefer deterministic/programmatic checks** (τ-bench-style end-state matching) and use the LLM judge only for the residual open-ended portion.
- **Hybrid two-stage is the strongest design:** Stage 1 = programmatic/state checks on atomic conditions; Stage 2 = trajectory-aware LLM judge only for conditions not programmatically checkable, with mandatory evidence-grounding. This bounds gaming exposure to the subset that genuinely needs judgment.
- **For the downgrade gate (success → partial when goal_met=False):** bias the rubric toward *requiring evidence of success*; treat unverifiable/narrated success as goal_met=False. Because the downgrade is triggered by the False class, optimize and monitor the judge for recall on goal_met=False (catch true failures) while watching false-downgrade precision.

### B. How the catalog datasets define ground-truth goal attainment
- **State-matching (most reusable definition):** τ-bench/τ²-bench (DB end-state == goal state), WebArena (programmatic state predicates), PayPal proxy-state. These define goal_met as an objective state property — the model to emulate.
- **Test-based:** SWE-bench Verified (unit tests pass) — objective, binary, human-validated solvability.
- **Reference-answer match:** GAIA (quasi-exact match), WebArena info tasks (exact / must_include / fuzzy).
- **Checkpoint/subgoal decomposition:** TheAgentCompany, AgentBoard — how to break a goal into atomic checkable items and handle partial credit.
- **Trajectory-error taxonomies (not goal_met, but useful for diagnosing the False class):** TRAIL, MAST, τ-bench auto-error-identification.
- **Judge-calibration sets:** AgentRewardBench (agent success), JudgeBench (objective pairwise) — reuse their reviewer protocols.

### C. Concrete annotation protocol for the binary goal_met gold set
1. **Goal decomposition:** For each task, author atomic success conditions, each paired with the observable evidence that verifies it (state predicate, tool output, file/DB change, or reference answer). goal_met=True iff all *required* conditions hold.
2. **Multi-axis label schema (collapsed to binary for the gate):** record (a) goal_met ∈ {True, False}; (b) graceful_failure / correct_impossible_report ∈ {True, False}; (c) partial_fraction ∈ [0,1]; (d) failure_mode (borrow MAST/TRAIL taxonomy); (e) evidence spans. The gate uses only goal_met; the rest supports calibration and error analysis.
3. **Impossible tasks:** Include a genuinely-impossible stratum (WebArena-style). Correct report of impossibility → goal_met=False + graceful_failure=True. Hallucinated completion of an impossible task → goal_met=False + graceful_failure=False. This keeps "behaved correctly" distinct from "achieved the goal." (Heed the WebArena-Verified finding that harnesses over-credit premature "N/A" — require evidence of adequate exploration before crediting a correct-impossible report.)
4. **Annotators & IAA:** ≥2 independent expert annotators per item; adjudicate disagreements; compute Krippendorff's α (target ≥0.8) on goal_met. Pilot on ~50 items, refine guidelines, then scale. Maintain a gold/calibration subset (~40% representative / 30% boundary / 20% edge / 10% impossible) for annotator onboarding and drift checks.
5. **Judge calibration:** Run GoalJudge over the gold set; report raw agreement, Cohen's κ, and especially precision/recall/F1 on goal_met=False; report ECE if using confidence; test for length/position/verbosity bias; and run a CoT-gaming red-team (inject fabricated-progress traces) as a dedicated stratum.
6. **Size & refresh:** 200–300 items for initial calibration; expand from production failures; re-calibrate periodically (e.g., quarterly gold-set refresh) and alert if κ drops below threshold.

## Recommendations
1. **Adopt a hybrid, outcome-grounded, trajectory-aware GoalJudge.** Programmatic/state checks first; LLM judge (seeing full trace + tool outputs) only for non-checkable conditions, with mandatory evidence-grounding and explicit instruction to distrust agent narration. *Threshold to change:* if red-team fabricated-progress traces flip >5–10% of verdicts, tighten evidence requirements or fall back to output+state-only judging.
2. **Build a ~250-item stratified, double-labeled gold set now**, reusing τ-bench/WebArena/TheAgentCompany schemas and definitions. Stratify by domain, difficulty, success/failure, and impossibility; oversample goal_met=False and impossible strata.
3. **Score correctly-reported-impossible and partial-completion as goal_met=False**, with separate graceful_failure and partial_fraction metadata so the gate stays strict while error analysis stays rich.
4. **Set the IAA gate at Krippendorff's α ≥ 0.8** on goal_met before trusting the gold set; below ~0.67, revise guidelines and add disambiguating examples.
5. **Calibrate and report precision/recall/F1 on the goal_met=False class plus ECE**, not just overall accuracy. Gate deployment on κ ≥ 0.6–0.8 vs. humans, leaning toward 0.8+ given the downgrade is consequential.
6. **Run a CoT-gaming red-team as a standing part of calibration**, tracking false-positive inflation as a first-class metric (the "Gaming the Judge" failure mode).
7. **Before fine-tuning for your ReAct pipeline**, freeze a held-out test split and do not iterate the judge prompt on it.

## Caveats
- **Maturity/consensus:** State-matching evaluation (τ-bench) and human-validated test-based eval (SWE-bench Verified) are mature and high-consensus. The 90/70 trajectory-vs-output agreement gap rests largely on one benchmark (DevAI) whose output-only baseline the authors acknowledge may be simplistic — treat the exact gap as indicative, not definitive. The CoT-gaming vulnerability is newly documented (Jan 2026) but large and well-isolated.
- **No off-the-shelf goal_met calibration set** maps cleanly to a generic ReAct pipeline; all catalog datasets require adaptation.
- **LLM-judge confidence is poorly calibrated**, so prefer agreement/κ and class-specific precision/recall over reported confidence; use ECE diagnostically only.
- **Contamination/benchmark decay** (e.g., SWE-bench Verified's Feb 2026 deprecation) means reused public tasks may overstate capability — prefer your own fresh tasks for the final calibration set.
- Several cited figures (sample-size rules, κ/α thresholds) are conventions, not laws; calibrate thresholds to your risk tolerance and the cost of an erroneous downgrade.
