# Ranking Hosted-API LLMs for Large-Scale ACT English Item-Bank Generation (as of July 2026)

## TL;DR
- **For maximum item quality (cost-no-object), the top author is Claude Opus 4.8** (Anthropic, $5/$25 per M tokens), with **GPT-5.5** (OpenAI, $5/$30) and **Gemini 3.1 Pro** (Google, $2/$12) as co-leaders; all three are reasoning models post-trained with RL, and Claude adds Constitutional AI/RLAIF that gives it the best prose discipline and instruction-following for Standard-English item writing.
- **For best value at 1,000+ item scale, the standout is Gemini 3.1 Pro** — it tops the independent Structured Output Benchmark on value accuracy (82.0%) at one-fifth to one-sixth the output price of the premium tier — with **DeepSeek V4** ($0.14/$0.28 Flash; $0.435/$0.87 Pro) and **GLM-5.2/GLM-4.7** as the cheapest credible open-weight-via-API options.
- **The right architecture is a two-model overgenerate-and-rank pipeline**: use a strong reasoning author (Claude Opus 4.8 or Gemini 3.1 Pro) to overgenerate items + distractors + rationales, and a cheaper fast model (Gemini 3.1 Pro/Flash or GPT-5.x-mini) plus deterministic validators as the LLM-as-judge/ranker — but keep a human in the loop, because Bitew et al. (2023, arXiv:2307.16338) found only "on average 53%" of LLM-generated distractors were rated by teachers as "high-quality, i.e., suitable for immediate use as is," and LLM judges systematically mis-score distractors.

## Key Findings

**1. The 2026 landscape has shifted to reasoning-by-default models.** Every frontier model now relevant to this task — GPT-5.5, Claude Opus 4.8/Sonnet 5, Gemini 3.1 Pro, DeepSeek V4, Qwen3.x, GLM-5.2, Kimi K2.x — is a reasoning model trained with reinforcement learning that produces internal chain-of-thought before answering. This matters for cost: hidden reasoning tokens are billed as output, so a reasoning model at "high" effort can consume 20K+ reasoning tokens on a single hard item, dwarfing the visible item text.

**2. Post-training methods now cluster into three recognizable families:**
- **RLHF + Constitutional AI / RLAIF (Anthropic).** Claude models use Constitutional AI: a supervised critique-revision loop against a written "constitution," followed by RL from AI feedback (RLAIF) using preference data labeled by a model against constitutional principles. Anthropic's more recent work adds synthetic-document fine-tuning (SDF) for constitutional adherence and RL over a broad set of safety-relevant environments. This pipeline is why Claude is repeatedly rated best on prose rhythm, tone consistency, and precise instruction-following.
- **RLVR / GRPO (reasoning models, especially open-weight Chinese labs).** DeepSeek-R1 — "DeepSeek-R1: Incentivizing Reasoning Capability in LLMs via Reinforcement Learning" (DeepSeek-AI, Guo et al.), published in *Nature* Vol. 645, pp. 633–638 (September 2025; arXiv:2501.12948) — established Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), showing "the reasoning abilities of LLMs can be incentivized through pure reinforcement learning (RL), obviating the need for human-labeled reasoning trajectories." It uses rule-based accuracy + format rewards, no learned reward model, no critic network. DeepSeek V4 folds R1's reasoning into a "thinking mode." Qwen3 uses a four-stage pipeline (long-CoT cold start → reasoning RL with GRPO → thinking-mode fusion → general RL); Qwen later introduced GSPO (Group Sequence Policy Optimization) to stabilize MoE RL. GLM-5 uses multi-task SFT → reasoning RL → agentic RL → general RL with cross-stage distillation.
- **RL with verifiable + model-based rewards (OpenAI, Google).** OpenAI's GPT-5 series is "trained to reason through reinforcement learning" per the GPT-5 and GPT-5.5 system cards; o-series/GPT-5 also use Deliberative Alignment (the model reasons over a written safety spec). Gemini 2.5/3 increased RL compute and use "verifiable rewards and model-based generative rewards"; Gemini's LearnLM variant adds RLHF specifically for pedagogical instruction-following.

**3. Instruction-following / structured-output reliability is the single most task-critical axis, and it does not track model size or headline intelligence.** The independent Structured Output Benchmark (SOB, JigsawStack, arXiv:2604.25359, April 2026) evaluated 21 models and found nearly all clear 95%+ JSON schema compliance, but value accuracy (exact leaf-value match) tops out at only 83.0% on text. On the text leaderboard, the leaders were **GLM-4.7 (0.830), Qwen3.5-35B (0.828), GPT-5.4 (0.825), Gemini-2.5-Flash (0.822)**, with the unified/complexity-weighted view putting **Gemini-3.1-Pro first at 82.0%**, then GLM-5.1 (80.6%), GLM-4.7 (80.4%), Qwen3.5-35B (80.1%). Critically, several open-weight models beat frontier closed models on value accuracy, and the "Perfect Response Rate" (every field correct) was far lower than JSON pass rate (e.g., GPT-5.4 at 0.999 JSON pass but 0.486 perfect responses) — meaning subtle field-level errors, not malformed JSON, are the real failure mode for multi-field item generation.

**4. On instruction-following benchmarks, the field is tightly bunched at the top.** On the IFEval-focused leaderboards (mid-2026), Qwen3.5-27B leads at 0.950, with Qwen3.7 Plus/Max, GPT-5.4, and Claude (Mythos/Opus-class) all in the 94-95% band; Gemini 3.1 Pro is cited around IFEval 95. The academic caution is that IFEval is prone to overfitting; the IFEval-FC paper (arXiv:2509.18420) showed even GPT-5 and Claude 4.1 Opus "frequently fail to follow basic formatting rules" when format constraints are embedded in function-call schemas. Practical implication: use provider-native strict/structured-output modes (OpenAI strict JSON schema, constrained decoding) rather than relying on prompt-only JSON.

**5. Reasoning/knowledge benchmarks relevant to "exactly one defensible key + plausible distractors" have largely saturated at the top.** On GPQA Diamond, GPT-5.5 (~94.0%), Gemini 3.1 Pro (94.1-94.3%), and Claude Opus 4.8 (93.6%) are in a statistical three-way tie; MMLU is saturated (~92%). MMLU-Pro sits around 86-88% for the leaders. For grammar-item generation this means all three frontier families comfortably exceed the reasoning threshold needed to keep a single correct answer defensible; the differentiator is writing quality and instruction adherence, not raw reasoning horsepower. Open-weight models (DeepSeek V4 Pro, Qwen3.x, GLM-5.2, Kimi K2.6) trail by only a few points on GPQA (~71-72 for DeepSeek V4 Pro).

**6. Writing quality favors Claude, then Gemini.** Practitioner and Arena-based rankings consistently put Claude Opus-class first on prose rhythm, subtext, and tone consistency, with Gemini 3.1 Pro leading raw creative-writing Arena Elo and GPT-5.x strongest on structured, format-following writing. For a product that itself must be grammatically impeccable and understand subtle usage/punctuation/rhetoric distinctions, Claude Opus 4.8 is the safest author, Gemini 3.1 Pro the best value author.

**7. The educational-item-generation literature gives clear, specific guidance:**
- **GPT-4/GPT-4o is the consistently best-validated frontier generator** in peer-reviewed work. A health-professions systematic review + network meta-analysis (Yu et al.; PROSPERO CRD42025608775) found no significant difference in the quality of distractors generated by ChatGPT 4 compared to humans (MD −0.10; 95% CI −0.24, 0.04; GRADE: VERY LOW), and likewise for relevance (MD −0.13; 95% CI −0.44, 0.18) and clarity (MD −0.03; 95% CI −0.15, 0.10); whereas "MCQs generated by Llama 2 performed worse than humans with regards to question clarity" (MD −1.21; 95% CI −1.60, −0.82) and distractor quality (MD −1.50; 95% CI −2.03, −0.97), and ChatGPT 3.5 was worse than both Llama 2 and ChatGPT 4 on clarity and distractor quality. (No rigorous head-to-head of GPT-5/Claude/Gemini on English/grammar item generation exists yet — a genuine gap.)
- **Overgenerate-and-rank is the key method** — Scarlatos, Feng, Smith, Woodhead & Lan, "Improving Automated Distractor Generation for Math Multiple-choice Questions with Overgenerate-and-rank," Proc. 19th BEA Workshop (NAACL 2024), pp. 222–231 (arXiv:2405.05144): overgenerate n=10 distractors, then rank with a model "trained via direct preference optimization (DPO) on pairwise preference pairs that compare the relative portion of students selecting one distractor over the other" to pick the top 3. It lifted a 7B Mistral model to GPT-4-level distractor quality; on GPT-4 CoT it improved proportional match 31.05→32.25 and weighted proportional 35.03→36.89.
- **Only about half of LLM distractors are usable as-is.** Bitew et al. (2023, arXiv:2307.16338) found "on average 53% of the generated distractors presented to the teachers were rated as high-quality, i.e., suitable for immediate use as is," using retrieval of question-bank items as in-context examples.
- **LLMs struggle to anticipate real student misconceptions** and produce plausible-but-flawed items. Scarlatos et al. note "LLM-generated distractors still do not match the quality of human-authored ones in reflecting student errors" and "strongly recommend that educators… review the quality of LLM-generated distractors thoroughly." Feng et al. (2024, arXiv:2404.02124) advise the tool "should be adopted as an auxiliary tool" with "careful review by educators." For grammar specifically, the 2024 CLiC-it English-grammar cloze study found the main failure was key/distractor ambiguity: "some generated distractors are interchangeable with the key."
- **LLM-as-judge has a documented, task-specific bias for this exact use case.** The D-GEN paper (arXiv:2504.13439) found that even when instructed to score incorrect distractors highly, "GPT occasionally penalized distractors simply for being incorrect. Among 700 instances, 123 received scores of 1 or 2, and among them, 32 instances (26.02%) were deducted due to the incorrectness of the distractors." More broadly, LLM judges exhibit position, verbosity, and self-preference biases; rubric-based scoring with human calibration is the recommended mitigation.

**Cost to generate 1,000+ multi-field items — rough model.** Assume each item requires ~1,500 input tokens (system prompt + few-shot exemplars, heavily cacheable) and produces ~1,200 visible output tokens (stem, 4 options, key, per-option rationale, per-distractor hint ladder). For a reasoning author add ~2,000-6,000 hidden reasoning tokens billed as output at "medium" effort.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro author (~4,000 output tokens/item incl. thinking):** ~$0.048 output + ~$0.003 input ≈ **$0.05/item → ~$50 for 1,000 items** (before batch/cache discounts).
- **Claude Opus 4.8 author (~4,000 output tokens/item):** ~$0.10 output + input ≈ **$0.10/item → ~$100 for 1,000 items**; batch mode halves this.
- **GPT-5.5 author:** output at $30/M makes it ~$0.12+/item → **$120+ for 1,000 items**; token-efficiency partially offsets.
- **DeepSeek V4 Flash validator/ranker (overgenerate 10 candidates, rank):** at $0.28/M output, ranking passes cost cents per item; **a full overgenerate-and-rank validation sweep over 1,000 items is well under $10.**

The dominant cost driver is the author's output tokens (including hidden reasoning), not input — so caching the large few-shot exemplar block (all providers offer ~90% cache discounts) and capping reasoning effort/`max_output_tokens` are the highest-leverage cost controls.

**Long-context / few-shot adherence.** All frontier options now offer ≥1M-token context (Gemini 3.1 Pro up to 2M; DeepSeek V4, GLM-5.2, Qwen3.x Max, GPT-5.5, Claude Opus/Sonnet all 1M). This comfortably holds a large few-shot exemplar set of ACT English items plus style guides. Gemini's 2M window and Claude's 1M-at-standard-price are the most headroom; note Gemini Pro applies a price step above 200K tokens and Claude's newer tokenizer produces ~30% more tokens per text, inflating real cost.

## Recommendations

**Tiered picks:**

1. **Top pick for maximum item quality (cost-no-object): Claude Opus 4.8 as author.** Its Constitutional-AI/RLHF post-training produces the most grammatically disciplined, tonally consistent prose and the tightest adherence to complex multi-constraint item-writing rubrics — exactly what Standard-English-conventions items demand. Run it with extended thinking on, strict output schema, and batch mode (50% discount) for the bulk run. Co-leaders GPT-5.5 (if you want the strongest native strict-JSON-schema tooling) and Gemini 3.1 Pro (if you want frontier quality at a fraction of the cost) are defensible substitutes.

2. **Best value / cost-efficient pick for bulk 1,000+ generation: Gemini 3.1 Pro.** It is the independent SOB value-accuracy leader (82.0%), has the largest context for few-shot exemplars, sits in the GPQA/IFEval top tier, and costs $2/$12 — roughly 5-6× cheaper on output than the premium tier. For an even cheaper bulk floor, GLM-4.7/GLM-5.2 (open-weight, top SOB value accuracy, ~$0.40-1.40 input) or DeepSeek V4 Flash ($0.14/$0.28) are the value extremes, with the caveat that Chinese-hosted first-party APIs carry data-residency/compliance considerations — route them through AWS Bedrock or Azure Foundry if student data or IP sensitivity matters.

3. **Recommended two-model overgenerate-and-rank strategy:**
   - **AUTHOR (generator):** Claude Opus 4.8 (quality-max) or Gemini 3.1 Pro (value). Overgenerate — for each item spec, produce ~8-10 candidate distractor sets + rationales + hint ladders, exploiting Scarlatos et al.'s finding that overgenerate-and-rank materially raises distractor quality.
   - **VALIDATOR / JUDGE / RANKER:** a *different-family*, cheaper fast model — Gemini 3.1 Pro/Flash or GPT-5.x-mini or DeepSeek V4 Flash — running rubric-based scoring (Haladyna/Item-Writing-Flaws rubric) plus deterministic gates (JSON-schema validation, exactly-one-key check, distractor-≠-key uniqueness, grammaticality/style linters). Use a *different* model family for judging than for authoring to avoid self-preference bias.
   - **Deterministic gates first, LLM judge second:** because SOB shows field-level value errors slip through valid JSON, and D-GEN shows LLM judges wrongly penalize distractors for being incorrect (26.02% of low-scored cases), put schema/uniqueness/answer-key validators ahead of the LLM judge, and constrain the judge to a rubric rather than open-ended quality scoring.
   - **Human-in-the-loop sign-off** on a sampled fraction, escalating to full review for any item the judge flags — non-negotiable given only ~53% as-is usability (Bitew et al. 2023) and the key/distractor-ambiguity failure mode specific to grammar items.

**Which model for which role:**
- **Best AUTHORS:** Claude Opus 4.8 > Gemini 3.1 Pro > GPT-5.5 (quality); Claude Sonnet 5 / Qwen3.x for cheaper bulk authoring.
- **Best JUDGES/VALIDATORS:** Gemini 3.1 Pro (top value accuracy, cheap), GLM-4.7/GLM-5.2 (top open-weight value accuracy, cheapest), GPT-5.x-mini (strong strict-schema). Always cross-family from the author.

**Benchmarks/thresholds that would change these recommendations:**
- If a rigorous head-to-head of GPT-5.5/Claude/Gemini on *English/grammar item generation* is published (currently a gap), re-rank authors on that direct evidence.
- If your internal eval shows the author's field-level value accuracy on your schema drops below ~95% perfect-response rate, add constrained decoding or split the item into multiple smaller structured calls.
- If bulk cost exceeds budget, shift authoring to Gemini 3.1 Pro or an open-weight model and reserve Claude Opus 4.8 only for items the judge flags as borderline.
- If data-residency/compliance is a hard constraint, drop first-party Chinese APIs and use Bedrock/Azure-hosted equivalents or Western frontier models only.

## Caveats
- **Fast-moving prices and versions.** All pricing and version numbers reflect mid-2026 aggregator and provider-doc snapshots and change frequently; several figures (especially Chinese-lab and third-party-gateway prices, and some 2026 model versions) come from secondary trackers rather than first-party pages and should be re-verified against official pricing before committing budget. Some very recent model versions and their benchmark numbers are vendor-reported or preprint-stage and contested (e.g., SWE-bench Verified vendor self-reports run far higher than Scale's standardized SWE-bench Pro).
- **Benchmark-to-task gap.** IFEval, MMLU-Pro, GPQA, and SWE-bench are proxies; none directly measures grammar-test-item quality. IFEval is known to overfit, and even top models fail embedded format constraints (IFEval-FC). Treat all leaderboard numbers as directional and run your own task-specific eval set of ACT English items before rollout.
- **The educational-AIG evidence is mostly on math and older models.** The strongest validated generator in the literature is GPT-4/GPT-4o (health-professions NMA, PROSPERO CRD42025608775); there is no peer-reviewed head-to-head of current frontier models on English/grammar item generation, so author rankings here rest on general writing/instruction-following evidence, not task-specific studies.
- **LLM-as-judge is not a substitute for human review.** Documented judge biases (position, verbosity, self-preference) and the D-GEN finding that judges penalize distractors for being incorrect mean the validator gates should be deterministic where possible and the LLM judge rubric-constrained, with human sign-off retained.
- **Reasoning-token cost is easy to under-budget.** Hidden reasoning tokens bill as output; a single high-effort call can cost far more than the visible item. Cap reasoning effort and `max_output_tokens`, and budget per-workload rather than per-request.
